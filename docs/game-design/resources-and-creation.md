# Resources and creation

Status: settled progression principles; named lineages, resource properties, recipes, tier counts and numerical balance remain to be designed. See the [world vision](world-vision.md) and [delivery roadmap](roadmap.md).

## Player projects drive progression

Players explore because they want to evolve and create in different domains: fight new enemies, cast new spells, build new machines, construct homes and invent combinations of those activities. Discovery reveals possibilities that can become personal goals.

Progression branches into a tree that becomes more intricate as the player develops. Cross-domain combinations connect its branches into a graph. Tiers describe comparable levels of creation potential and processing capability. They do not prescribe a single sequence of increasingly superior materials or an order in which every player must master every domain.

Within a tier, materials serve different purposes. None should be inherently superior to all its peers. A material's suitability depends on the intended creation and the properties it needs. Advancement opens new functions, combinations and scales of work as well as greater power where appropriate.

Every material in a tier should have a worthwhile reason to seek it. A player pursuing broad mastery needs to explore to obtain the breadth of resources; a specialist can pursue the subset their chosen projects require. Required combinations should be understandable from the creation's function, rather than an unrelated collection checklist.

## Material roles

Wood, stone, metals, gems and bones all have purposes. Define a material through relevant properties and uses, then test it against its peers. Potential design dimensions include toughness, flexibility, weight, heat behavior, conductivity, precision, magical affinity, storage and stability. The exact property model must emerge from real gameplay needs.

For each introduced material, answer:

1. Where can a player find it, and how can they recognize or investigate that source?
2. What can they achieve with accessible techniques?
3. What further techniques or combinations reveal its advanced potential?
4. Which useful purpose makes it worth choosing over materials of the same tier?
5. Which later creations still have a reason to use it?

Earlier resources continue to contribute to advanced creations through their properties and availability. A powerful machine can still need stone containment, wooden structures, metal connections or gem focusing. Avoid making such ingredients busywork; their contribution should be visible in the result or its behavior.

## Bones are the highest natural resource tier

Colossus bones have the greatest natural creation potential. Lineages differ in their material inheritance: some favor weaponry, some technology, some magic, and some provide fragile material that readily yields shards. Multiple lineages can occupy this highest tier without one universally dominating the others.

Accessible fragile bone supports societies with simpler tools and can be useful to players early. Advanced treatment or assembly can reveal more of its potential later. Access to a fragment does not require immediate mastery of every use of that material. This distinction reconciles easy shard gathering with bones' place at the top of natural resources.

Use suitable bone as a decisive contribution to exceptional creations. Other material families retain complementary roles. Top-tier potential must be experienced in what players can make; presentation and lore alone cannot establish it.

These brainstorm examples illustrate possible breadth. Their names and behaviors are provisional:

| Working lineage | Possible material character | Possible applications |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crownbacks | Tough, resilient, hard to shape | Weapons, armor, gates, strong machine components. |
| Stormwings | Responds to vibration or stores force | Mechanisms, instruments, actuators, unusual weapons. |
| Veilborn | Holds or transforms magical influence | Spell structures, enchantments, magical machinery. |
| Petalhorns | Fragile, readily separated into useful flakes | Simple shard tools, ornaments, precise assemblies, magical lattices. |

These associations are tendencies. Inventive cross-domain uses are desirable. The actual first lineage and its properties must be selected when its content slice is scoped.

## Extraction and abundance

A colossal skeleton contains an enormous amount of valuable material. Its scale must participate in the balance instead of becoming an unlimited supply of identical finished components.

Candidate balancing dimensions include anatomy, preservation, material condition, extraction technique, processing requirements and the quantity needed for a project. Weathered ribs, dense joints, teeth and preserved inner structures could serve different purposes. Select and test a small set of rules for the first release; do not require every candidate dimension at once.

Distinguish usable supply from visual volume clearly. Players should be able to learn what a visible part yields and why, without arbitrary invisible exceptions that turn resource-bearing bones into decoration. A large deposit can legitimately support large construction ambitions.

Test these questions before expanding a bone family:

- Does the first accessible skeleton erase reasons to investigate other lineages or material families?
- Does useful extraction reveal choices and techniques, or only a long repetitive mining task?
- Can loose shards have meaningful early uses while advanced creations demand additional mastery?
- Can a specialist pursue a project without completing unrelated branches?
- Can builders use bone at architectural scale without trivializing every advanced resource requirement?
- Does modifying a skeleton persist consistently with ordinary world edits and any settlement reactions?

Bone depletion, regeneration, trade supply, structural consequences and replenishment policies remain open balance decisions. Do not assume indestructible landmarks or infinite replenishment. Define those rules in the slice that first makes them relevant.

## Composable creation domains

Combat, engineering, magic and building share the material economy while developing distinct functions. Exact crafting interfaces, recipe syntax and research mechanisms remain open.

| Domain | Growth to support | Cross-domain possibilities |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Combat | New weapon behaviors, protection and answers to different threats | Mechanized weapons, enchanted equipment, player-built defenses. |
| Engineering | New transformations, motion, control and scales of production | Machines operating magical effects or supporting an unusual habitat. |
| Magic | New effects, affinities, control and persistent workings | Enchanted structures, material processing, effects incorporated into machines. |
| Building and habitation | New forms, environments, material expression and useful spaces | Workshops, defended homes, submerged settlements, aerial transport structures. |

Teach dependable rules through examples and readable feedback. Named recipes can introduce those rules while modular interactions allow additional inventions. Each first domain slice should demonstrate at least one useful alternative or combination to its example creation. Arbitrary scripts, every imaginable effect and unlimited simulation complexity are not prerequisites for this creative direction.

## Exploration and habitation

Distribute useful material roles across environments so travel opens meaningful choices. Give players observable leads: recognizable remains, habitat patterns, exposed deposits, crafted samples, maps or local knowledge as those systems arrive. Resource availability must support a learnable search, with enough access to sustain projects.

A frontier's resources should enable something locally useful as well as creations worth bringing elsewhere. Consider production, shelter, travel and continued supply when designing habitation. A player may want to maintain a workshop underground, live beside an ocean resource, or build among the skies.

Balance travel friction against that desire. Evaluate access, preparation, carrying materials, establishing a home and returning after a mistake together. Early releases can offer modest forms of these activities; the promised mature frontier must support an ongoing way of life.

## Balance evidence

For every resource release, document the material roles, expected acquisition and processing paths, first useful outputs and intended cross-domain combinations. Record what remains experimental.

Playtest a new arrival, a specialist, a builder and a player seeking broad mastery. Observe whether they can form a project, find its ingredients, understand their choices and create a result. Test whether one option dominates all peers, earlier materials lose their uses, or one deposit collapses exploration incentives. Numerical prices, yields, costs and encounter tuning should follow these observations and may change without rewriting the world vision.
